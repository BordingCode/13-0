Where the numbers come from

Player cards

RATING is HLTV career rating, DUELS is K/D ratio, MILEAGE is rounds played, and LEVEL is how strong the teams were that the player actually played for. Each is shown as a percentile against the other 624 players in the pool.

OVR blends the four. A career rating alone is misleading — a 1.17 in a tier-2 league is not a 1.17 against Astralis — so LEVEL carries real weight.

Roles and weapons

Roles are Liquipedia's documented ones. Weapon fit is a design rule on top of them: an AWP in an AWPer's hands is the team's whole plan, and the same rifle handed to an entry fragger instead of a lurker is worth a little less. Nobody's kill counts are invented.

Team strength

Every team is rated by an Elo over 68,932 real matches, where the unit is the round, not the win — in Counter-Strike the scoreline is the dominance signal. A 13-0 moves a rating far more than a 13-11, and beating a strong team counts for more than beating a weak one. Series that only recorded map counts are calibrated onto the same scale, where a 2-0 reads as roughly a 13-5.

The projected finish

Before you press play, the game runs this exact 16-team field 400 times and reports the middle half of the outcomes. It is a forecast, not a result — a roster projected 5th–8th wins the whole thing sometimes, and the tournament you actually play is rolled fresh.

The bit that isn't real

The tournament itself. Your drafted team never existed, so its run is simulated: each round is a coin weighted by the rating gap between the two teams. Real teams keep their real strength, but no real result is being reported.
